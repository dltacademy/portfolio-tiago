The story

Two trainings that almost never go together.

  1. People

    First, I wanted to understand why people do what they do.

    I trained in clinical and conversational hypnosis, in behavior analysis and in scientific NLP, and went deep into Alfred Adler’s psychology, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy and social engineering. I learned to hear what a person asks for and what they need. They are almost never the same thing.

    Before I build, I find the right problem.

  2. Systems

    Then, how things work on the inside.

    I studied systems architecture, distributed and complex systems and cellular automata, and created my own method, DFM: every problem becomes small functions, each with what it must do and how it can fail. With it I designed Drayker, an open supersystem, from the kernel to the councils.

    What I build can grow, and does not break at the first surprise.

  3. In practice

    So I tested everything on myself.

    I built my own management system in Obsidian, with its own plugin, and started living in it: the day, the week, the quarter and the projects in the same cycle of plan, execute and review. For three years I have traveled the world working this way, with no office and no fixed team.

    I only offer what already works in my own life.

  4. Live

    And I put it up, end to end.

    DLT Academy is the most complete example: the portal, six tools, the guides, the campaigns and the first paid product, sold through Stripe and delivered automatically. Along the way I wrote and published a book in English and Portuguese, built my own video bench, and put AI agents to work under supervision.

    You talk to one person, who understands the problem, the people and the code.

  5. Now

    Now I do this for your project.

    An idea that needs to become a product, an operation that needs a system, a decision that weighs. That is where I come in: to understand, design and deliver it working.

How I work

One method, from the problem to delivery.

I use DFM, a method I created: the problem becomes small functions, each with what it must do and how it can fail, and each piece only goes in once it is proven.

  1. Understand

    What needs to happen, for whom, and what stands in the way today. Before any tool.

  2. Model

    The system as functions and connections, with the risks of each in view and what is left out stated.

  3. Build and prove

    Piece by piece, with real tests and checks on each one. What does not pass does not go in.

  4. Deliver

    Live, documented and in the hands of the people who will use it, with the reason behind each decision.
